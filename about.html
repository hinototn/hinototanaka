About

I’m Hinoto Tanaka, a geography student at Middlebury College in Vermont. I have Japanese heritage and have lived in Detroit, Nagoya, Suzhou, Hong Kong and Tokyo; my home is now in Fishers, Indiana. I work with cartography, GIS and spatial research, and I’m interested in visual storytelling, particularly the research, design and development of maps.

Feel free to reach out via LinkedIn or email.

The stamp

In Japan, a personal seal, called a hanko or inkan, is used in place of a signature. People press it on official documents, bank forms and letters, and it stands for the person in a way a handwritten signature doesn’t quite.

The stamp next to my name was carved for me by my mother. It reads 央杜, which is how my name, Hinoto, is written. 央 means center, and 杜 means forest, especially the sacred grove that surrounds a Shinto shrine. Together they mean something like a sacred forest at the center.
